# Requirements

Apple first: every item is met on a physical iPhone 17 Pro before any Android work starts.

## Equipment

- R1. One pack: the engine bay of a 2010 VW Gol Trend 1.6.
- R2. Six top-level parts: coolant reservoir, power steering reservoir, brake fluid reservoir, battery, fuse box, engine.
- R3. The engine holds two parts: valve cover and intake manifold.

## Viewer

- R4. The splat renders with Metal inside a React Native view.
- R5. One finger orbits and a pinch zooms.
- R6. Tapping a part highlights it; tapping empty space clears the highlight.
- R7. A highlighted part is tinted sky blue (#38BDF8) and everything else is slightly dimmed.
- R8. Highlighting the engine also highlights the parts inside it.
- R9. The camera animates to frame the highlighted part.
- R10. Orbiting holds 60 fps with up to 1.5M splats.

## Guide

- R11. A card shows the picked part's name and a short explanation.
- R12. Three procedures: check the coolant, check the brake fluid, check the power steering fluid.
- R13. Each procedure step highlights and frames its parts.
- R14. Next, back and repeat work by voice and by button.

## Instructor

- R15. Spoken free questions are answered on device by Apple Foundation Models.
- R16. The instructor answers only from the pack and says when it does not know.
- R17. The instructor can show a part and move through a procedure.

## Offline

- R18. The app works in airplane mode from its first launch, with the pack bundled.

## Delivery

- R19. A 60 to 90 second video recorded on the iPhone in airplane mode.
- R20. A TestFlight build.
- R21. A public repository whose README, architecture notes and ADRs explain every decision.

## Later

- Android, pack downloads and the remote instructor, only once every item above is met.
